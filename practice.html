Free PTCE Practice Questions (with Answers & Explanations)

Twenty sample questions for the pharmacy technician exam (PTCE), five from each of the four exam domains. Try each one, then open Show answer to see the correct choice and why it's right.

Questions are written to the current PTCE content outline and fact-checked against federal rules and drug references. They are study material, not official exam questions.

Medications

Question 1 · Easy

What is the generic name for Zetia?

  1. A colesevelam
  2. B fenofibrate
  3. C ezetimibe
  4. D niacin
Show answer

Answer: C. ezetimibe

Zetia is ezetimibe, which lowers LDL cholesterol by blocking intestinal cholesterol absorption. Colesevelam (Welchol) is a bile acid sequestrant, fenofibrate is a fibrate, and niacin is a B vitamin once widely used for lipids.

Question 2 · Easy

Why should aspirin be avoided in children and teenagers who have a viral illness such as the flu or chickenpox?

  1. A Risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome
  2. B Risk of Reye's syndrome
  3. C Risk of gingival hyperplasia
  4. D Risk of orange urine
Show answer

Answer: B. Risk of Reye's syndrome

Aspirin use during viral illnesses in children and teens is linked to Reye's syndrome, a rare but potentially fatal disorder affecting the brain and liver. Gingival hyperplasia (phenytoin), orange urine (rifampin), and Stevens-Johnson syndrome are associated with other drugs, not with this aspirin warning.

Question 3 · Medium

A patient taking lisinopril is newly prescribed a diuretic. Which diuretic poses the greatest risk of hyperkalemia in combination?

  1. A furosemide
  2. B hydrochlorothiazide
  3. C spironolactone
  4. D metolazone
Show answer

Answer: C. spironolactone

Spironolactone is a potassium-sparing aldosterone antagonist, and ACE inhibitors also raise potassium by reducing aldosterone, so the combination can cause dangerous hyperkalemia. Loop and thiazide diuretics such as furosemide, hydrochlorothiazide, and metolazone tend to lower potassium.

Question 4 · Medium

Which over-the-counter medication can raise serum lithium concentrations toward toxic levels?

  1. A acetaminophen
  2. B ibuprofen
  3. C loratadine
  4. D famotidine
Show answer

Answer: B. ibuprofen

NSAIDs such as ibuprofen reduce renal lithium clearance and can raise levels into the toxic range. Acetaminophen is generally the preferred OTC analgesic for patients on lithium.

Question 5 · Hard

Which drug can cause Stevens-Johnson syndrome or toxic epidermal necrolysis, with a much higher risk in patients who carry the HLA-B*1502 allele?

  1. A levetiracetam
  2. B gabapentin
  3. C lithium
  4. D carbamazepine
Show answer

Answer: D. carbamazepine

Carbamazepine carries a boxed warning for serious skin reactions (SJS/TEN). The risk is strongly linked to HLA-B*1502, found mainly in people of Asian ancestry, so genetic screening is recommended before starting it in at-risk patients. Levetiracetam, gabapentin, and lithium do not carry this warning.

Federal Requirements

Question 6 · Easy

Under the federal Controlled Substances Act, which schedule contains drugs with a high potential for abuse and NO currently accepted medical use in the United States?

  1. A Schedule I
  2. B Schedule II
  3. C Schedule III
  4. D Schedule IV
Show answer

Answer: A. Schedule I

Schedule I drugs such as heroin and LSD have a high potential for abuse and no currently accepted medical use, so they cannot be prescribed. Schedule II is the tempting choice because those drugs also have a high abuse potential, but they do have accepted medical uses (e.g., oxycodone, fentanyl).

Question 7 · Easy

How often must a pharmacy take a complete inventory of all controlled substances under federal law?

  1. A Every 6 months
  2. B Every year
  3. C Every 2 years
  4. D Every 3 years
Show answer

Answer: C. Every 2 years

Federal law requires a biennial (every 2 years) inventory of all controlled substances on hand. Every 3 years is tempting because that is how often the pharmacy's DEA registration is renewed, which is a separate requirement.

Question 8 · Medium

A pharmacy arranges for expired controlled substances in its stock to be destroyed. Which DEA form records the destruction?

  1. A DEA Form 41
  2. B DEA Form 106
  3. C DEA Form 222
  4. D DEA Form 224
Show answer

Answer: A. DEA Form 41

DEA Form 41 is the registrant record of controlled substances destroyed. Form 106 is the tempting distractor because it also involves drugs leaving inventory, but it is only for theft or significant loss.

Question 9 · Medium

What is the CMEA 30-day purchase limit for pseudoephedrine base bought by mail order or from a mobile retail vendor?

  1. A 3.6 g
  2. B 7.5 g
  3. C 9 g
  4. D 15 g
Show answer

Answer: B. 7.5 g

Mail-order and mobile retail vendor sales are limited to 7.5 g of base per customer per 30 days. 9 g is tempting because it is the 30-day limit for purchases made in a retail store.

Question 10 · Hard

A pharmacy can supply only part of the quantity on a Schedule II prescription because it is out of stock. Under federal rules, within what time must the remaining quantity be supplied before the balance is void?

  1. A 24 hours
  2. B 72 hours
  3. C 7 days
  4. D 30 days
Show answer

Answer: B. 72 hours

When the pharmacy cannot supply the full quantity, the remainder may be dispensed within 72 hours; after that the prescriber must be notified and no further quantity may be supplied. 30 days is tempting because it applies to partial fills requested by the patient or prescriber under CARA.

Patient Safety & Quality Assurance

Question 11 · Easy

What is the main purpose of Tall Man lettering, such as hydrOXYzine vs. hydrALAZINE?

  1. A To highlight the differing letters of look-alike drug names so they are less likely to be confused
  2. B To flag every high-alert medication on the shelf
  3. C To mark controlled substances on shelf labels
  4. D To show the brand name hidden inside the generic name
Show answer

Answer: A. To highlight the differing letters of look-alike drug names so they are less likely to be confused

Tall Man lettering capitalizes the letters that differ between look-alike names so the eye catches the difference during selection and verification. Flagging high-alert drugs is tempting because both are safety labels, but high-alert status is shown with separate alerts and storage, not Tall Man lettering.

Question 12 · Easy

Which type of strategy does ISMP rank as MOST effective at preventing medication errors?

  1. A Forcing functions and constraints, such as oral syringes that cannot connect to IV lines
  2. B Staff education and training
  3. C Rules and policies
  4. D Reminders and checklists
Show answer

Answer: A. Forcing functions and constraints, such as oral syringes that cannot connect to IV lines

ISMP's hierarchy ranks forcing functions and constraints highest because they make the error physically impossible rather than relying on people to remember. Education is tempting because it is widely used, but it depends on human memory and is among the least effective strategies.

Question 13 · Medium

A prescriber wants to order one-half milligram. Which way of writing the dose follows safe-writing rules?

  1. A .5 mg
  2. B 0.50 mg
  3. C 1/2 mg
  4. D 0.5 mg
Show answer

Answer: D. 0.5 mg

A leading zero is required before a decimal less than 1 (0.5 mg) so the decimal point isn't missed and read as 5 mg. '0.50 mg' is tempting because it includes the leading zero, but the trailing zero is also prohibited because it can turn 0.5 into 50 if the decimal is missed.

Question 14 · Medium

VAERS is co-managed by which two federal agencies?

  1. A The FDA and the DEA
  2. B The CDC and CMS
  3. C The FDA and HRSA
  4. D The CDC and the FDA
Show answer

Answer: D. The CDC and the FDA

VAERS is jointly run by the CDC and the FDA. 'FDA and HRSA' is tempting because HRSA runs the Vaccine Injury Compensation Program, but VAERS itself is co-managed by the CDC and FDA.

Question 15 · Hard

Vincristine has been fatal when given by the wrong route. How does ISMP recommend dispensing it?

  1. A Diluted in a minibag (not a syringe) and labeled for intravenous use only, fatal if given by other routes
  2. B Undiluted in a syringe labeled 'For intravenous use only'
  3. C In a syringe with a distinctive colored cap to tell it apart from intrathecal drugs
  4. D In a syringe stored separately from intrathecal medications until administration
Show answer

Answer: A. Diluted in a minibag (not a syringe) and labeled for intravenous use only, fatal if given by other routes

Vincristine is fatal if given intrathecally, and dispensing it in a minibag makes it physically hard to connect to a spinal needle. A syringe is tempting because it is a standard way to give small IV doses, but syringes are what allowed the fatal intrathecal errors.

Order Entry & Processing

Question 16 · Easy

A prescription reads: Amoxicillin 250 mg/5 mL. Sig: 1 tsp PO TID x 10 days. How many milliliters should be dispensed total?

  1. A 75 mL
  2. B 100 mL
  3. C 150 mL
  4. D 200 mL
Show answer

Answer: C. 150 mL

1 teaspoon (tsp) = 5 mL. TID means three times a day. 5 mL x 3 = 15 mL per day. 15 mL/day x 10 days = 150 mL total.

Question 17 · Easy

A stock bottle's expiration date reads '06/2027.' Through what date may the product be used?

  1. A June 1, 2027
  2. B May 31, 2027
  3. C December 31, 2027
  4. D June 30, 2027 (the last day of the month)
Show answer

Answer: D. June 30, 2027 (the last day of the month)

When an expiration date shows only a month and year, the product is good through the last day of that month. June 1, 2027 is tempting because it is the first date in the stated month, but the convention is the end of the month.

Question 18 · Medium

Translate the sig: i tab po q4-6h prn pain

  1. A Take 1 tablet by mouth every 4 to 6 hours as needed for pain
  2. B Take 4 to 6 tablets by mouth every hour as needed for pain
  3. C Take 1 tablet by mouth 4 to 6 times a day for pain
  4. D Take 1 tablet under the tongue every 4 to 6 hours for pain
Show answer

Answer: A. Take 1 tablet by mouth every 4 to 6 hours as needed for pain

'i' = one, 'tab' = tablet, 'po' = by mouth, 'q4-6h' = every 4 to 6 hours, 'prn' = as needed. '4 to 6 times a day' is tempting, but 'q4-6h' sets the interval between doses, not the number of doses per day.

Question 19 · Medium

Which part of a traditional prescription contains the drug name, strength, and ingredient amounts?

  1. A The inscription
  2. B The signa
  3. C The superscription
  4. D The subscription
Show answer

Answer: A. The inscription

The inscription is the body of the prescription naming the drug, strength, and ingredients. The subscription is tempting because it follows the inscription, but it gives directions to the pharmacist, such as the quantity to dispense.

Question 20 · Hard

Which of the following does federal law (FD&C Act section 503(b)(2)) require on the label of a dispensed prescription?

  1. A The drug's NDC
  2. B The prescription's serial number
  3. C The patient's date of birth
  4. D The pharmacy's DEA number
Show answer

Answer: B. The prescription's serial number

Federal law requires the dispenser's name and address, the serial number, the date, the prescriber's name, the patient's name (if stated), and the directions and cautionary statements. The NDC is tempting because it is printed on many labels, but that is a state or business requirement, not part of 503(b)(2).
